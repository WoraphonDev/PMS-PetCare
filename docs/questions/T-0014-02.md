# T-0014-02 · F08 13-digit checksum algorithm and input normalisation
- Status: open
- Raised by: claude · T-0014 (card preparation) · 2026-10-22
- Context: foundation#rule-F08 says "เบอร์มือถือ `^0\d{9}$` หรือเลขผู้เสียภาษี 13 หลักที่ checksum ถูก" but does not name the algorithm, whether any first digit is allowed, or whether formatted input (dashes/spaces) is normalised. The DB check `branch_promptpay_id_format` (`^(0[0-9]{9}|[0-9]{13})$`) checks shape only; its comment says the checksum is validated in the use case. `org.update` `taxId` explicitly does **not** check a checksum (#contract-org), so there is no existing helper to reuse.
- Question: Which checksum and normalisation should F08 use?
- Options:
  (A) Thai 13-digit ID / tax-ID check digit (mod 11): with digits d1…d13, `d13 = (11 − (Σ_{i=1..12} d_i × (14 − i)) mod 11) mod 10`. Any first digit, including `0` (juristic-person tax IDs such as `0105…`). Input is trimmed only (§11); dashes, spaces inside, `+66` or other formats → `VALIDATION_FAILED`. Stored value is the trimmed digits.
  (B) Same checksum as A, but also strip dashes/spaces before validating and store the digits only.
  (C) Same as A, and additionally reject first digit `9` (unused personal ID prefix).
  — แนะนำ: A. It is the standard algorithm for both citizen IDs and Revenue Department tax IDs, and trim-only matches §11 and the DB regex without inventing a normalisation rule. Mobile numbers follow `^0\d{9}$` exactly as written (no further carrier-prefix rule).
- Answer: 2026-10-22 · human chose (A) in chat, **on condition that the formula is checked against an official source before it is written into the spec** ("หากแหล่งอ้างอิงขัดกับข้อเสนอหรือยืนยันไม่ได้ ให้แจ้งฉัน ห้ามเดา"). Decided and recorded in foundation#rule-branch-promptpay-format: trim leading/trailing whitespace only; no stripping of inner dashes/spaces; no `+66` conversion; mobile = F08 regex `^0\d{9}$`; 13 digits with no first-digit restriction.
  Still open — the checksum formula itself. Reference check (claude, 2026-10-22):
  - Official, confirms the method exists but gives no formula: Revenue Department TIN sheet for the OECD (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/thailand-tin.pdf), Section II 2.4: digit 13 of Revenue-Department numbers (`099…`) "is the check digit, calculated using the same method applied to the 13-digit identification numbers issued by the Department of Provincial Administration". The same sheet says prefixes `100–999` are issued by the Department of Provincial Administration and `010–096` by the Department of Business Development, but does not say whether DBD numbers use that check digit.
  - Official, no formula: central data standard (data owner กรมการปกครอง) https://datastandard.m-society.go.th/datatable/index/1 — "หลักที่ 13 หมายถึง เลขตรวจสอบความถูกต้อง (CHECK DIGIT)… ตรวจสอบและคำนวณด้วยเครื่องคอมพิวเตอร์".
  - Formula (weights 13…2, mod 11, `(11 − r) mod 10`) found only in non-official sources, e.g. https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/49148, which cites the former page dopa.go.th/fop/pid13.htm (now HTTP 404; the archive copy could not be fetched from here).
  - Result: the mod-11 formula is **not confirmed by an official source**, and whether DBD juristic numbers (`010–096`, e.g. `0105…`) carry the same check digit is **not confirmed** either. Nothing contradicts option A, but per the condition above the formula is not written into the spec. Needed: an official document (กรมการปกครอง / กรมพัฒนาธุรกิจการค้า / กรมสรรพากร) giving the formula and its scope, or a human decision on how to proceed.
