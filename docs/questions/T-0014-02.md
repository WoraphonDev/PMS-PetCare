# T-0014-02 · F08 13-digit checksum algorithm and input normalisation
- Status: answered (human decision; formula not confirmed by an official source)
- Raised by: claude · T-0014 (card preparation) · 2026-10-22
- Context: foundation#rule-F08 says "เบอร์มือถือ `^0\d{9}$` หรือเลขผู้เสียภาษี 13 หลักที่ checksum ถูก" but does not name the algorithm, whether any first digit is allowed, or whether formatted input (dashes/spaces) is normalised. The DB check `branch_promptpay_id_format` (`^(0[0-9]{9}|[0-9]{13})$`) checks shape only; its comment says the checksum is validated in the use case. `org.update` `taxId` explicitly does **not** check a checksum (#contract-org), so there is no existing helper to reuse.
- Question: Which checksum and normalisation should F08 use?
- Options:
  (A) Thai 13-digit ID / tax-ID check digit (mod 11): with digits d1…d13, `d13 = (11 − (Σ_{i=1..12} d_i × (14 − i)) mod 11) mod 10`. Any first digit, including `0` (juristic-person tax IDs such as `0105…`). Input is trimmed only (§11); dashes, spaces inside, `+66` or other formats → `VALIDATION_FAILED`. Stored value is the trimmed digits.
  (B) Same checksum as A, but also strip dashes/spaces before validating and store the digits only.
  (C) Same as A, and additionally reject first digit `9` (unused personal ID prefix).
  — แนะนำ: A. It is the standard algorithm for both citizen IDs and Revenue Department tax IDs, and trim-only matches §11 and the DB regex without inventing a normalisation rule. Mobile numbers follow `^0\d{9}$` exactly as written (no further carrier-prefix rule).
- Answer:
  - 2026-10-22 (1) · human chose (A) in chat: trim leading/trailing whitespace only; no stripping of inner dashes/spaces; no `+66` conversion; mobile = F08 regex `^0\d{9}$`; 13 digits with no first-digit restriction. The formula was to be checked against an official source first.
  - 2026-10-22 (2) · after the reference check below, the human **approved the option-A formula as PMS validation policy without waiting for an official source**: with digits d1…d13, `S = Σ_{i=1..12} d_i × (14 − i)`, `checkDigit = (11 − (S mod 11)) mod 10`, accept when `d13 = checkDigit`. This is a human decision to unblock the work. It is **not** a claim that the formula or its scope (personal IDs, Revenue Department `099…`, DBD `010–096`) has been confirmed by a government agency. Spec: foundation#rule-branch-promptpay-format.
- Reference check (claude, 2026-10-22) — kept as found:
  - Official, confirms a check digit exists but gives no formula: Revenue Department TIN sheet for the OECD (https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/thailand-tin.pdf), §II 2.4: digit 13 of Revenue-Department numbers (`099…`) "is the check digit, calculated using the same method applied to the 13-digit identification numbers issued by the Department of Provincial Administration". It also says prefixes `100–999` come from the Department of Provincial Administration and `010–096` from the Department of Business Development, without saying whether DBD numbers use that check digit.
  - Official, no formula: central data standard (data owner กรมการปกครอง) https://datastandard.m-society.go.th/datatable/index/1 — "หลักที่ 13 หมายถึง เลขตรวจสอบความถูกต้อง (CHECK DIGIT)… ตรวจสอบและคำนวณด้วยเครื่องคอมพิวเตอร์".
  - Not readable from here: https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/49148 (HTTP 403; seen only as a search snippet citing the former page dopa.go.th/fop/pid13.htm, which now returns 404). The DBD PDF cited by python-stdnum, https://www.dbd.go.th/download/pdf_kc/s09/busin_2542-48.pdf, returns 404.
  - Read, **secondary source** (not official): python-stdnum (LGPL, open-source validation library), commit `006192e59be8ed6e08fd680256868a6c31eb2ba9`:
    - `stdnum/th/pin.py` — https://github.com/arthurdejong/python-stdnum/blob/006192e59be8ed6e08fd680256868a6c31eb2ba9/stdnum/th/pin.py — `calc_check_digit`: `s = Σ (2 − i)·d_i mod 11` (0-based i), check = `(1 − s) mod 10`. This is the same function as the approved formula: `(2 − i) ≡ (13 − i) (mod 11)` and `(1 − s) ≡ (11 − s) (mod 10)`.
    - `stdnum/th/moa.py` — https://github.com/arthurdejong/python-stdnum/blob/006192e59be8ed6e08fd680256868a6c31eb2ba9/stdnum/th/moa.py — states that DBD numbers (always starting with `0`) use "the same algorithm as in the Personal Identity Number".
    - `tests/test_th_pin.doctest`, `tests/test_th_moa.doctest`, `tests/test_th_tin.doctest` (same commit) — lists of numbers "found online and should all be valid" (the MOA list cites `https://vsreg.rd.go.th/VATINFOWSWeb/jsp/VATInfoWSServlet?`) plus explicit `InvalidChecksum` examples. These are the independent test vectors used in the T-0014 card.
    - Read, secondary, partial: https://charin-nawaritloha.github.io/excel-vba-notes/20180111_check_sum_id.html says only that digit 13 uses "modulus 11"; the formula itself is in an image that could not be read.
  - Consistency check (not used to derive expected results): all 111 listed vectors (49 starting with `0`) pass the approved formula; the listed `InvalidChecksum` examples fail it; and stdnum's formula and the approved formula agree on 200,000 random 13-digit strings.
  - Limits that remain true: a valid check digit only catches typing errors. It does not show that the number is registered for PromptPay, that an account exists, or who owns it. The juristic-person vectors come from an open-source test file that says they were found online (via the Revenue Department VAT service), not from a government list.
